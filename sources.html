Methodology

How this was built, and where every number came from.

Data sources

  • Campaign finance figures come from the Federal Election Commission's public API (api.open.fec.gov) and FEC.gov candidate/committee pages, queried directly against committee IDs C00935452 (Burck), C00952663 (Askar), C00901793 (Carbonara). Where a candidate had no locatable FEC committee for the FL-22 / 2026 cycle (Keiser, Evans, Hasdorff, Thompson), figures are drawn from contemporaneous news reporting and flagged as such.
  • Donor-level detail (donors.html) is pulled from FEC Schedule A itemized receipt records for committee C00935452, retrieved via the Schedule A endpoint of the FEC API.
  • Vote totals and percentages are drawn from the Florida Division of Elections / Florida Supervisor of Elections reporting as relayed by Marco News, and cross-checked against Florida Politics, the Palm Beach Post, Miami Herald, and an AAHOA election-results summary.
  • Biographical detail on each candidate comes from Ballotpedia, campaign websites, Wikipedia, and primary-source candidate Q&As published by the Palm Beach Post and News-Press ahead of the primary.
  • Outside spending / independent expenditure figures are drawn from Legis1's FL-22 race brief, which itself aggregates FEC independent-expenditure filings, supplemented by The Hill's reporting on Club for Growth Action's involvement.

Known limitations

  • Several candidates (Keiser, Evans, Hasdorff, Thompson) do not have a discoverable FEC committee page specific to the FL-22 2026 race at the time of research, despite news reports citing fundraising totals for them. This may reflect recent filings not yet processed by the FEC, filings under a different committee name, or reporting based on candidate/campaign self-disclosure rather than FEC filings. Where this is the case, it is noted directly on the relevant page.
  • The FEC's official candidate-totals figure for Burck's itemized individual contributions ($252,562.13) is slightly higher than the sum of individual transaction records retrievable via the public Schedule A API at time of research ($203,382 net of refunds); this is most likely explained by report-timing/amendment differences between the aggregated totals endpoint and the itemized schedule endpoint. The donor ledger presents the itemized transactions actually returned by the API.
  • Exact raw vote counts (as opposed to percentages) for each FL-22 GOP primary candidate were not independently located in a single authoritative table at time of research; percentages are corroborated across at least two independent sources for each candidate.
  • "Cost per point of vote share" is an analytical construct created for this site to enable apples-to-apples comparison across self-funded and donor-funded candidates. It is not a standard FEC or campaign-finance industry metric.
