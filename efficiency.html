The Investment Thesis

Dollar for dollar, David Burck was the most efficient candidate in the race.

Measured the way an investor would measure it — output (vote share) per dollar of capital deployed — David Burck delivered more primary performance per dollar than every candidate who finished ahead of him. He did it with a smaller checkbook, no self-funding, and no outside Super PAC riding in to help.

The pitch

If this is what he did on $255K, what could he do on $2.5M?

Casey Askar won this primary by loaning his own campaign $2.5 million — roughly 10x what David Burck raised entirely from individual supporters. Michael Carbonara spent $4.5 million to finish 2nd. Belinda Keiser reported over $3.2 million to finish 3rd. Burck finished 4th, ahead of three other candidates, on a campaign that was 100% individually funded and carried zero debt.

Extrapolating his demonstrated cost-per-point efficiency to the resource levels of the top three finishers is not a guarantee of a win — but it is a legitimate, evidence-based argument that David Burck is dramatically under-capitalized relative to his demonstrated ability to convert money into votes. A campaign that already knows how to win efficiently is exactly the kind of investment that compounds when properly funded.

The ask, in one line.

Every rival who beat David Burck in this primary did so with either a personal fortune or a national Super PAC behind them — not a better message, not more support on the ground, just more capital. Close that capital gap next cycle, and the fundamentals that already worked once — grassroots individual support, service-first biography, zero self-funding — start working at a scale that wins.
