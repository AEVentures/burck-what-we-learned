Every campaign's treasurer report, line by line.

Federal campaign committees are required to itemize every contribution over $200 on FEC Schedule A. This is that data, pulled directly from each committee's own filings — not summaries, the actual disclosed transactions, so you can see exactly who funded David and exactly who funded the field he was running against.

A note on how "self-funding" actually shows up in these filings.

When a candidate loans their own campaign money, FEC rules only require the campaign to disclose that the candidate personally provided the funds (Schedule A, receipt type "Loans Received from the Candidate" / "Personal Funds") — not the ultimate source of that money in the candidate's personal or business finances. If a candidate's income in a given period came from, say, a business deal, a consulting fee, or a liquidity event, and that money is then loaned into the campaign, it is disclosed as a "personal funds" loan — legally correct, but it means the true original source of that capital (and any expectations attached to it) never has to be itemized the way a $500 check from an individual donor does. That's a real, structural transparency gap in federal campaign finance law, not a flaw specific to any one candidate here — but it's worth knowing when you compare a "self-funded" war chest to a donor-funded one.
