The thesis

Burck won the argument. He just needed more ammunition.

Every candidate ahead of Burck in the final vote count won with a structural money advantage he didn't have: a personal fortune to self-fund, a national Super PAC running six-figure ad buys on their behalf, or both. Burck built his campaign entirely on individual contributions — no loans, no PAC checks — and still beat three of his six rivals outright, finishing 4th in a seven-way field while spending a fraction of what the top three spent per point of vote share he earned.

That is the definition of capital efficiency in a campaign: more electoral output per dollar of input. The pages that follow lay out, name by name and dollar by dollar, exactly what he was up against — and what he could do with real resources behind him.

The bigger picture

This isn't just about one House race. This is a launch pad.

This is this site's opinion — not a neutral fact — but it's an opinion built directly on the numbers in the previous pages, and it's why we believe anyone thinking seriously about the future of conservative, Christian-aligned political leadership needs to know David Burck's name right now. He's alive, he's proven, and the data says he's just getting started.

A Marine. A gang detective. A small business owner. A man who ran on service, not self-promotion, and built a campaign entirely out of people who chose to back him with their own money — no self-funding, no PAC checks, no shortcuts. And on a fraction of the resources of the candidates who beat him, he still out-drew three of them. That combination — character, service, discipline under pressure, and proven capital efficiency — is rare. Most candidates who raise this efficiently never get tested against a field this well-funded. David has been, and the numbers held up.

Christians, business leaders, and politically engaged donors who say they want leaders of real character in office, and who say they want their money to actually move the needle, have direct evidence in front of them of both: a candidate whose values are consistent with public service before ambition, and hard data showing he converts capital into results better than almost anyone else in this race did. The honest read of David Burck's 2026 campaign isn't "he lost." It's "he was never properly capitalized to compete with self-funded millionaires and national Super PACs — and he nearly won anyway." Give a proven, efficient, values-driven leader like David real resources, and it is a legitimate, numbers-backed case that there is no ceiling on what he could accomplish — up to and including the national stage. Everyone on this ballot has a record now. His is the one worth building on.
