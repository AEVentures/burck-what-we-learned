This is not opposition research. It's a postmortem — a clear-eyed look back at David Burck's 2026 run in Florida's 22nd Congressional District, what it actually proved about him as a candidate, and what it means for what comes next. David raised $255,073, entirely from individual donors, with zero PAC money and zero personal loans, in a field that raised and spent roughly $22.5 million combined — most of it self-funded by millionaires or backed by outside Super PACs. He still out-drew three better-funded rivals at the ballot box. This site lays out exactly what happened, dollar for dollar, and makes the case that a candidate this efficient, properly capitalized, is worth serious, sustained investment — not just for the next House race, but for whatever comes after it.

The thesis

Burck didn't lose the argument. He lost the arms race.

Every candidate ahead of Burck in the final vote count won with a structural money advantage he didn't have: a personal fortune to self-fund, a national Super PAC running six-figure ad buys on their behalf, or both. Burck built his campaign entirely on individual contributions — no loans, no PAC checks — and still beat three of his six rivals outright, finishing 4th in a seven-way field while spending a fraction of what the top three spent per point of vote share he earned.

That is the definition of capital efficiency in a campaign: more electoral output per dollar of input. The pages that follow lay out, name by name and dollar by dollar, exactly what he was up against — and what he could do with real resources behind him.

The bigger picture

This isn't just about one House race

This is where the postmortem turns into an argument. It's this site's opinion — not a neutral fact — but it's an opinion built directly on the numbers in the previous pages, and it's why we think anyone thinking seriously about the future of conservative, Christian-aligned political leadership should be paying attention to David Burck right now.

A Marine. A gang detective. A small business owner. A man who ran on service, not self-promotion, and built a campaign entirely out of people who chose to back him with their own money — no self-funding, no PAC checks, no shortcuts. And on a fraction of the resources of the candidates who beat him, he still out-drew three of them. That combination — character, service, discipline under pressure, and proven capital efficiency — is rare. Most candidates who raise this efficiently never get tested against a field this well-funded. David has been, and the numbers held up.

Christians, business leaders, and politically engaged donors who say they want leaders of real character in office, and who say they want their money to actually move the needle, have direct evidence in front of them of both: a candidate whose values are consistent with public service before ambition, and hard data showing he converts capital into results better than almost anyone else in this race did. The honest read of David Burck's 2026 campaign isn't "he lost." It's "he was never properly capitalized to compete with self-funded millionaires and national Super PACs — and he still nearly did." Give a proven, efficient, values-driven leader like David real resources, and it is a legitimate, numbers-backed question whether there is a ceiling on what he could accomplish — up to and including the national stage.
